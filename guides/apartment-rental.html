🏠 How to Rent an Apartment in Japan (For Foreigners)

Life in Japan Guide · Housing

Renting your first apartment in Japan can feel like a maze of paperwork, guarantors, and up-front fees. Here's what to expect, step by step, from preparing your documents to picking up the keys.

The 6 Steps at a Glance

  1. Prepare Your Documents — Residence card, proof of income, and more
  2. Search for a Property — Real estate agencies and listing portals
  3. Viewing & Application — Tour the property and submit an application
  4. Screening Process — Guarantor company or personal guarantor review
  5. Sign & Pay Initial Costs — Deposit, key money, agency fee, and more
  6. Final Contract & Key Handover — Contract briefing and move-in

Step 1: Prepare Your Documents

  • Residence Card (or Passport) — Required as ID; used to confirm your visa status and length of stay.
  • Proof of Income — Employment certificate, pay slips, withholding tax slip, or bank balance certificate.
  • Emergency Contact / Guarantor Info — A local emergency contact or joint guarantor may be required.
  • Personal Seal or Signature — More companies now accept a signature in place of a personal seal (hanko).
Tip: Prepare extra copies of your documents. If you're not confident in Japanese, look for an agency with English support.

Step 2: Search for a Property

  • Find a Foreigner-Friendly Agency — Agencies used to working with foreign tenants make the process smoother.
  • Use Rental Listing Portals — SUUMO, at home, and GaijinPot Housing let you filter by your criteria.
  • Organize Area, Budget & Requirements — Decide on rent, distance from the station, layout, and total upfront costs.
  • Check for "Foreigner OK" Listings — Some landlords restrict listings to Japanese tenants only — confirm in advance.
Tip: A common rule of thumb is rent under one-third of your monthly income. Budget 4-6 months' rent for initial costs.

Step 3: Viewing & Application

  • Tour the Property In Person — Check natural light, noise, and the surrounding area, not just photos.
  • Submit an Application Form — Include your name, employer, income, and emergency contact details.
  • Apply Early for Popular Listings — Good properties can be gone within a few days.
  • You Can Still Cancel at This Stage — Withdrawing an application is usually fine before screening begins.
Tip: During the viewing, also check sunlight direction, plumbing, storage space, and mobile signal strength.

Step 4: Screening Process

  • Guarantor Company Review — Using a guarantor company instead of a personal guarantor is now common.
  • A Personal Guarantor May Be Needed — Some landlords still ask for a Japan-based guarantor, like a friend or boss.
  • Income & Visa Status Check — Stable income and valid residency status are key screening factors.
  • Screening Takes a Few Days to a Week — You can keep browsing other listings while you wait for results.
Tip: If you can't find a guarantor, ask your agency about guarantor companies experienced with foreign tenants.

Step 5: Sign & Pay Initial Costs

  • Security Deposit (Shikikin) — Covers restoration costs at move-out; part is often refunded when you leave.
  • Key Money (Reikin) — A non-refundable thank-you payment to the landlord, typically 1-2 months' rent.
  • Agency Fee — Usually capped at about one month's rent plus tax, paid to the agency.
  • Advance Rent & Fire Insurance — Often paid together in a lump sum at the time of contract signing.
Tip: Total initial costs typically run 4-6 months' rent. Confirm accepted payment methods (bank transfer, cash) in advance.

Step 6: Final Contract & Key Handover

  • Receive the Important Matters Explanation — A licensed agent explains the contract terms — listen carefully.
  • Sign & Seal the Lease Agreement — Review the lease term, renewal fees, and cancellation conditions closely.
  • Receive Your Keys — Once the contract is complete, collect the keys on your scheduled move-in date.
  • Set Up Your Utilities — Don't forget to arrange electricity, gas, water, and internet service.
Tip: Ask questions before you sign. Contracts are usually in Japanese only, so request translation help if you need it.

Common Challenges for Foreigners

  • No joint guarantor available — Finding a Japanese guarantor can be difficult; a guarantor company often solves this.
  • Few foreigner-friendly listings — Some landlords restrict rentals due to language or cultural concerns.
  • Understanding Japanese documents — Contracts and important-matters explanations are often Japanese-only.
  • Visa status & length of stay limits — A short remaining visa period or certain visa types can make screening stricter.

Key Takeaways

  • Prepare your residence card and proof of income well in advance.
  • A foreigner-friendly agency makes the whole process much smoother.
  • A guarantor company can often resolve the lack of a personal guarantor.
  • Take your time reviewing the contract and important-matters explanation before signing.
  • Budget roughly 4-6 months' rent for total initial costs.
