📦 Postal & Parcel Delivery in Japan

Life in Japan Guide · Everyday Life

Japan's delivery system is famously precise and convenient once you know how it works — from reading a delivery notice slip to rescheduling a missed delivery in seconds. Here's how to get your mail and packages right, every time.

3 Things to Get Comfortable With

  1. Receiving Mail & Packages — Understanding notices, signatures, and your mailbox
  2. Redelivery Made Easy — What to do when you miss a delivery
  3. Sending Packages — Domestic and international shipping basics

Receiving Mail & Packages

  • Your Mailbox — Most apartments have individual mailboxes in the entrance area — check it regularly.
  • Delivery Notice Slips (Fuzai Hyo) — Left behind when you miss a delivery, with a tracking number and redelivery instructions.
  • Signatures for Registered Mail — Some mail requires a signature or hanko stamp — a simple signature is usually fine.
  • Intercom & Entry Issues — Apartment auto-lock entrances can confuse delivery drivers — leaving clear entry instructions helps.
Tip: Write your name in katakana next to the Roman alphabet on your mailbox and doorbell — it helps delivery drivers match you to the label.

Redelivery Made Easy

  • Call the Number on the Slip — Every delivery notice includes a phone number and tracking number for redelivery.
  • Use the Carrier's App or Website — Japan Post, Yamato, and Sagawa all offer online redelivery scheduling.
  • Scan the QR Code — Many notice slips include a QR code that takes you straight to a redelivery form.
  • Pick Up at a Convenience Store — Many carriers let you redirect a package to a nearby convenience store instead.
Tip: Redelivery services are usually available in English online, even if the notice slip itself is in Japanese only.

Sending Packages

  • Convenience Store Drop-Off — Many konbini accept prepaid parcels for Yamato or Sagawa — fast and simple.
  • Post Office Counters — Best for registered mail, EMS, and anything needing customs forms.
  • Sending Internationally (EMS) — Fill out a customs declaration and check restricted item rules before sending.
  • Size & Weight Pricing — Domestic rates are based on box dimensions and weight — smaller boxes save money.
Tip: Keep your receipt and tracking number until the package is confirmed delivered — it's your proof if something goes missing.

Common Challenges for Foreigners

  • Missing the delivery window — Drivers often only attempt delivery once before leaving a notice slip.
  • Language barrier on the phone — Redelivery hotlines are sometimes Japanese-only — the apps are usually easier.
  • Confusing apartment entry systems — Auto-lock buildings can prevent drivers from reaching your door.
  • Customs delays on international packages — Items from abroad may be held for customs inspection or import tax payment.

Key Takeaways

  • Check delivery notice slips carefully — they have everything you need for redelivery.
  • Use each carrier's app or website for the easiest redelivery experience.
  • Convenience stores are a fast, reliable option for both pickup and sending.
  • Keep tracking numbers and receipts until delivery is confirmed.
  • Write your name in katakana near your mailbox to help drivers find you.
