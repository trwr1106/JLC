📱 Getting a SIM Card, Phone Plan & Home Wi-Fi in Japan

Life in Japan Guide · Mobile & Internet

Setting up a phone contract and home internet is one of the first practical hurdles after arriving in Japan. Here's a step-by-step walkthrough of getting connected, from choosing a carrier to configuring your home Wi-Fi.

The 6 Steps at a Glance

  1. Prepare Your Documents — Residence card, passport, and a payment method
  2. Choose a Carrier Type — Major carrier, MVNO, or prepaid SIM
  3. Pick a Plan — Data-only, voice + data, or home Wi-Fi
  4. Visit a Store or Apply Online — In-store signup or online application
  5. Sign the Contract & Activate — ID verification and SIM activation
  6. Set Up Your Device & Home Wi-Fi — APN settings and router installation

Step 1: Prepare Your Documents

  • Residence Card (Zairyu Card) — Required as proof of ID and visa status for any phone contract.
  • Passport — Sometimes requested alongside your residence card for verification.
  • Payment Method — A Japanese bank account or credit card is usually required for billing.
  • Current Phone (if bringing one) — Check it is SIM-free / unlocked before switching carriers.
Tip: If you don't have a Japanese bank account or credit card yet, a prepaid SIM is a good short-term option.

Step 2: Choose a Carrier Type

  • Major Carriers — NTT Docomo, au, and SoftBank offer wide coverage and in-store English support.
  • MVNOs (Budget Carriers) — Rakuten Mobile, IIJmio, and mineo offer lower prices, mostly online signup.
  • Prepaid SIM — No contract or bank account needed — ideal for short stays or a quick start.
  • eSIM Providers — Airalo and similar services let you activate a plan digitally in minutes.
Tip: MVNOs are often 30-50% cheaper than major carriers but may have fewer physical stores for support.

Step 3: Pick the Right Plan

  • Data-Only SIM — Best if you already have a way to make calls (e.g. LINE, WhatsApp).
  • Voice + Data Plan — Includes a Japanese phone number for calls and texts.
  • Pocket Wi-Fi (Mobile Router) — A portable hotspot — no fixed-line installation required.
  • Home Fiber Internet — Best for long-term stays; usually needs installation and a contract term.
Tip: Compare data caps carefully — many budget plans throttle speed heavily after a monthly data limit.

Step 4: Visit a Store or Apply Online

  • Visit a Carrier Store — Staff help you complete the form; some locations offer English support.
  • Book an Appointment — Popular stores in cities can have long waits — reserve a time slot online.
  • Apply Online — Many MVNOs and eSIM providers let you sign up entirely from your phone.
  • Bring All Required Documents — Missing paperwork is the most common reason for delays.
Tip: Look for stores labeled with an English-support sign, or check the carrier's website for English-speaking locations.

Step 5: Sign the Contract & Activate

  • Identity Verification (eKYC) — In-store or via app photo/video verification of your residence card.
  • Review Contract Terms — Check the minimum contract period and any early cancellation fees.
  • Receive Your SIM or eSIM — Handed to you in-store, or delivered by mail within a few days.
  • Number Porting (MNP) — If keeping an existing number, request a transfer code (MNP) in advance.
Tip: Photograph or save a copy of your contract — it's useful if you need to cancel or make changes later.

Step 6: Set Up Your Device & Wi-Fi

  • Insert the SIM / Install the eSIM — Follow the QR code or instructions provided at signup.
  • Configure APN Settings — Enter the carrier's APN details if data doesn't connect automatically.
  • Install Your Home Router — A technician visit is often scheduled for fiber internet setup.
  • Test Your Connection — Confirm calls, data, and Wi-Fi are all working before leaving the store.
Tip: Save your carrier's customer support number and APN settings somewhere offline in case you need to reset your device.

Common Challenges for Foreigners

  • No bank account or credit card yet — Many contracts require Japanese billing details; a prepaid SIM avoids this.
  • Credit or residency screening — Some carriers check residency length or run a credit check for voice plans.
  • Language barrier — Contract terms are often Japanese-only outside major English-support stores.
  • Home internet needs a guarantor — Some fiber providers require a local contact or longer contract commitment.

Key Takeaways

  • Bring your residence card, passport, and a payment method to any signup.
  • Prepaid SIMs and eSIMs are the fastest options with no bank account needed.
  • MVNOs are cheaper but offer less in-person, English-language support.
  • Check contract length and cancellation fees before signing.
  • Book an appointment ahead of time to avoid long waits at busy stores.
