🗑️ How to Sort Garbage in Japan (For Foreigners)

Life in Japan Guide · Everyday Life

Waste separation is one of the first things that trips up new residents in Japan — rules vary by city, and getting it wrong can mean your bag sits uncollected for a week. Here's how to sort your garbage correctly, no matter where you live.

4 Main Garbage Categories

  1. Burnable Garbage — Food scraps, paper waste, and other combustible items
  2. Non-Burnable Garbage — Small appliances, ceramics, glass items, and metal objects
  3. Recyclables — PET bottles, cans, glass bottles, newspapers, and cardboard
  4. Oversized Garbage — Furniture, bicycles, and large appliances needing a reservation

How the Categories Compare

Burnable Garbage

  • Kitchen & Food Waste — Drain excess liquid before placing scraps in the bag.
  • Non-Recyclable Paper Waste — Used tissues, small paper scraps, and dirty paper products.
  • Small Wood & Garden Waste — Fallen leaves and small branches, cut to the required size.
  • Use Your City's Designated Bags — Most municipalities require specific bags sold at local stores.
Tip: Check your ward or city's official calendar or app — collection days vary by neighborhood.

Non-Burnable Garbage

  • Small Appliances — Hairdryers, toasters, and similar items — remove batteries first.
  • Glass & Ceramics (Not Bottles) — Broken dishes, cups, and other non-bottle glass or ceramic items.
  • Metal Objects — Pots, pans, and small metal parts that aren't recyclable cans.
  • Wrap Sharp Items Safely — Wrap knives or broken glass in paper and label it clearly as dangerous.
Tip: Batteries and light bulbs often need separate disposal — check your municipality's specific rules.

Recyclables

  • PET Bottles — Rinse the bottle, then remove the cap and label before recycling.
  • Cans & Glass Bottles — Rinse thoroughly before placing them in the designated collection net.
  • Newspapers & Cardboard — Flatten and bundle with string, keeping each type separate.
  • Plastic Packaging — Some cities collect this separately — rinse before sorting it out.
Tip: Rules for plastic packaging vary by city — some combine it with burnable trash, others don't.

Oversized Garbage

  • Schedule a Pickup — Call or apply online — most cities require an advance reservation.
  • Buy a Disposal Fee Sticker — Available at convenience stores or supermarkets near you.
  • Attach the Sticker & Set It Out — Place the item outside on your scheduled collection date.
  • Fees Vary by Item & Size — Check your municipality's official price list for exact costs.
Tip: Book your pickup well in advance — popular collection dates can fill up quickly in busy areas.

Common Challenges for Foreigners

  • Rules vary drastically by city — What counts as burnable in one ward may be non-burnable in another.
  • Complex recyclable sorting — A single item may need to be split into several separate categories.
  • Finding the correct designated bags — The required color, size, and brand of bag differs by municipality.
  • Missing the collection day — Garbage left out on the wrong day may sit uncollected for a week.

Key Takeaways

  • Check your municipality's official sorting guide as soon as you move in.
  • Buy the correct designated bags — required in most areas.
  • Rinse and separate recyclables carefully by type.
  • Oversized items need a reservation and a paid disposal sticker.
  • When in doubt, ask a neighbor or check your ward's multilingual guide or app.
