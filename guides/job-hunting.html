💼 Job Hunting in Japan

Life in Japan Guide · Work

Finding and landing a job as a foreign resident in Japan involves its own conventions — from where to actually look, to a resume format that looks nothing like what you're used to, to an interview culture with its own unwritten rules. Here's a practical breakdown of all three.

3 Keys to a Successful Job Search

  1. Where to Look — Finding job openings that are actually open to foreign applicants
  2. Application Materials — Building a resume that matches Japanese expectations
  3. Interview & Culture — Making a strong impression the Japanese way

Where to Find Jobs

  • Hello Work (Government) — Japan's public employment service — free to use, with some English support in major cities.
  • Foreigner-Focused Job Boards — Sites like GaijinPot and Daijob list roles specifically open to international candidates.
  • LinkedIn & Professional Networks — Increasingly used in Japan, especially for tech, finance, and international roles.
  • Recruitment Agencies — Bilingual agencies can match you to roles and help negotiate on your behalf.
Tip: Check that a job listing's visa sponsorship policy matches your situation before applying — it saves time for everyone.

Building Your Application

  • Rirekisho (Resume) — A standardized personal history form — format and photo requirements matter more than in Western resumes.
  • Shokumu Keirekisho — A separate document detailing your work history and achievements in more depth.
  • Photo Requirements — A recent, formal photo is expected — casual or cropped selfies are not appropriate.
  • Tailoring for Each Application — Japanese employers expect materials adjusted to each specific company and role.
Tip: Many convenience stores and photo studios offer quick, properly formatted resume photos for a few hundred yen.

Interview Culture

  • Punctuality Matters — Arrive 5-10 minutes early — being late, even slightly, is taken seriously.
  • Formal Language (Keigo) — Polite, respectful language is expected throughout the interview.
  • Business Attire — Conservative, formal dress is the norm, even for casual company cultures.
  • Common Questions — Expect questions about long-term commitment, teamwork, and why you want to work in Japan.
Tip: Practicing self-introductions (jiko shoukai) out loud beforehand makes a noticeable difference in confidence.

Common Challenges for Foreigners

  • Language barrier in interviews — Even strong Japanese speakers can struggle with formal business language under pressure.
  • Uncertain visa sponsorship — Not all companies are experienced with sponsoring or transferring work visas.
  • Different interview expectations — Japanese interviews often emphasize fit and long-term commitment over pure skill.
  • Lack of "Japanese work experience" — Some employers hesitate without local experience, even with strong overseas credentials.

Key Takeaways

  • Combine Hello Work, foreigner-focused boards, and agencies for the widest reach.
  • Format your rirekisho and shokumu keirekisho to Japanese conventions.
  • Practice formal language and punctuality before every interview.
  • Confirm visa sponsorship policies early to avoid wasted applications.
  • Tailor your application materials for each company rather than reusing one version.
