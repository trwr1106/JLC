🎓 Student & Study Support in Japan

Life in Japan Guide · Students

Studying in Japan involves its own set of paperwork, deadlines, and decisions — from choosing a school to keeping your visa in good standing. Here's a practical guide to the three pillars of studying in Japan.

3 Pillars of Studying in Japan

  1. Enrollment — Choosing a school and completing the application process
  2. Scholarships — Finding funding to make studying in Japan more affordable
  3. Student Visa — Getting your visa and staying in status while you study

School Enrollment Process

  • Choose Your School Type — Language schools, vocational schools (senmon gakko), and universities all have different requirements.
  • Apply Directly or Through an Agent — Many schools accept direct applications; agents can help navigate the process for a fee.
  • Submit Required Documents — Academic transcripts, certificates, financial proof, and a statement of purpose are typically required.
  • Receive Your Certificate of Eligibility — Your school applies for this (COE) on your behalf once you're accepted — it's required for your visa.
Tip: Application deadlines for April enrollment are often 6-9 months in advance — start early, especially for university programs.

Finding & Applying for Scholarships

  • MEXT Scholarship — Japan's government scholarship, often covering tuition, a monthly stipend, and airfare.
  • JASSO Scholarships — Offered through many universities for both short-term and long-term students.
  • University-Specific Scholarships — Many schools offer their own tuition reductions or stipends for international students.
  • Private Foundations & Sponsors — Organizations like Rotary and various private foundations also fund international students.
Tip: Apply as early as possible — many scholarships have deadlines nearly a year before the program starts.

Student Visa Basics

  • Certificate of Eligibility (COE) First — Your school applies for this on your behalf after you're accepted.
  • Apply for Your Visa — Take your COE to a Japanese embassy or consulate in your home country.
  • Part-Time Work Permission — Apply for "shikaku gai katsudo" to legally work up to 28 hours per week.
  • Renew Before It Expires — Visit immigration to extend your student visa before your current one runs out.
Tip: Keep your attendance rate high — poor attendance can affect visa renewal and future work visa applications.

Common Challenges for International Students

  • Complex, lengthy paperwork — COE applications and visa paperwork can take months and involve many documents.
  • Scholarship competition — Popular scholarships like MEXT are highly competitive with early deadlines.
  • Balancing work and attendance limits — Part-time work is capped at 28 hours/week, and poor attendance can jeopardize your visa.
  • Post-graduation visa transition — Switching from a student visa to a work visa requires careful timing and planning.

Key Takeaways

  • Start your school application 6-9 months before your intended enrollment date.
  • Apply for scholarships as early as possible — deadlines are often a year in advance.
  • Your school handles your Certificate of Eligibility; you handle the visa at the embassy.
  • Register for part-time work permission if you plan to work while studying.
  • Keep your attendance strong — it matters for visa renewal and future opportunities.
