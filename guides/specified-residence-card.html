🪪 Japan's New "Specified Residence Card" System (2026)

Life in Japan Guide · Immigration & Admin Procedures

Starting June 14, 2026, Japan's Immigration Services Agency is rolling out a brand-new type of residence card — the Specified Residence Card (特定在留カード) — that merges your residence card and My Number card into a single piece of plastic. If you're a mid- to long-term resident, this is one of the biggest changes to immigration paperwork in years, and it's worth understanding before your next renewal.

What's Actually Changing

Right now, most foreign residents carry two separate cards: a residence card (issued by Immigration) and a My Number card (issued by your city hall). The Specified Residence Card combines both into one card, so your immigration status and your resident registration are handled through a single document instead of two separate systems.

A few other details changed on the card itself:

  • Your period of stay, the type of permission, the date it was granted, and the card's issue date are no longer printed on the face of the card — they're stored only in the IC chip for privacy.
  • For permanent residents and Highly Skilled Professional (Type 2) holders, the card's validity was extended from a flat 7 years to your 10th birthday after the card was issued.
  • Photos are now required for children aged 1–15, not just adults.

Who Can Get One — And Is It Mandatory?

The Specified Residence Card is available to mid- to long-term residents and special permanent residents who are registered in the resident registry (juki daicho) of their city or ward. Getting one is optional — you can still choose to keep carrying a separate residence card and My Number card if you prefer, or if you never applied for a My Number card in the first place.

How to Apply

You can apply for a Specified Residence Card in two ways:

  1. At a regional immigration bureau — at the same time as a status-of-residence change, a period-of-stay renewal, a permanent residency application, or a residence card reissuance.
  2. At your city or ward office — at the same time as filing a moving-in notification (tenkyo todoke).

If you just want to switch to the new card without any other procedure pending, you can apply for a "reissuance by exchange request" (交換希望による在留カードの再交付) together with the Specified Residence Card application.

You'll need: the application form, a PIN-setting form, one photo, your passport, and your current residence card. Regional bureaus started accepting applications on June 15, 2026 (the first business day after launch).

Important Things to Know Before You Apply

  • It takes longer. Issuance takes about 10 days longer than a regular residence card — there's no same-day issuance for the Specified Residence Card.
  • The My Number function has its own expiry. The My Number card function on your Specified Residence Card is only valid through your current period of stay. If your immigration paperwork (like a renewal) isn't finished by then, you'll need to visit your city/ward office separately to extend the My Number function's validity before your residence period ends.
  • You have to reapply each time. If you want to keep using a combined card long-term, you need to apply for a new Specified Residence Card every time your regular residence card is reissued (renewal, status change, etc.) — it doesn't carry over automatically.
  • Fees. Your first application from June 14, 2026 onward is generally free; later applications may carry a fee depending on the circumstances.

Don't Forget: Regular Residence Card Renewals Still Work the Same Way

Whether or not you switch to the new card, the basic rules for renewing your period of stay haven't changed:

  • You can apply from 3 months before your residence period expires (if your period of stay is 6 months or longer). In special cases — study abroad, overseas business trips, pregnancy, medical treatment — you may be able to apply even earlier.
  • Application fee: ¥6,000 at the counter, or ¥5,500 if you apply online.
  • Standard processing time is 2 weeks to 1 month, so don't wait until the last minute.
  • Apply at the regional immigration bureau covering your address, or call the Foreign Residents' General Information Center at 0570-013904 if you have questions.

Tips

  • Set a reminder for 3 months before your residence card expires — that's your window to start the renewal process.
  • If you're not sure whether the Specified Residence Card is worth the extra hassle, there's no rush: it's optional, and you can always apply later at your next renewal.
  • Keep your current residence card and passport handy for any procedure — you'll need both no matter which route you take.

Still have questions?

Immigration paperwork is confusing even for long-term residents — and this is a brand-new system, so a lot of people (including some city hall staff) are still getting up to speed.
