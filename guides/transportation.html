🚆 Getting Around Japan: Bus, Subway, Train & IC Cards

Life in Japan Guide · Transportation

Japan's public transportation is famously punctual and easy to use once you know the basics. Here's how bus, subway, and train compare, plus everything you need to know about IC cards, commuter passes, and student discounts.

3 Ways to Get Around

  • Bus — Covers local areas in detail. Fares are relatively low but can be affected by traffic.
  • Subway — Connects the center of a city. Highly punctual with frequent service — the backbone of daily commuting.
  • Train (JR / Private Lines) — Best for longer distances between cities and suburbs, with fast connections.

Bus vs. Subway vs. Train

What Is an IC Card?

An IC card is a rechargeable tap-and-go card accepted across most of Japan's public transit — and beyond.

  • Tap and go — Just touch the card to the reader at a ticket gate or on a bus — the fare is calculated automatically.
  • Nationwide interoperability — Major cards like PASMO, Suica, ICOCA, and Kitaca all work across most of Japan.
  • Also works as e-money — Use the same card to pay at convenience stores, vending machines, and many restaurants.
  • No need to buy paper tickets — Skip the fare calculation and ticket machine lines for a smoother trip.
Tip: In most major cities, it doesn't matter which IC card you have — it will work on almost any bus, subway, or train.

Getting & Topping Up Your Card

  • Buy one at a station machine — A 500 yen deposit plus your chosen top-up amount gets you started.
  • Mobile versions are available — Load the card function onto your smartphone and top up from an app.
  • Topping up (charging) — Add funds with cash at station machines or at convenience store registers.
  • Watch your balance — Running low can cause an error at the gate — check your balance regularly.
Tip: If you stop using the card, you can return it at a station counter for a refund of the deposit (a small fee may apply).

Commuter Passes (Teiki)

  • Unlimited rides on a fixed route — Covers a set route, such as home to office, for one flat price.
  • Choose 1, 3, or 6 months — The longer the period, the lower the effective monthly cost.
  • Often covered by your employer — Many companies fully or partially reimburse commuter pass costs.
  • Can be loaded onto an IC card — PASMO or Suica can carry your pass so you only need one card.
Tip: You can usually get on and off at any station within your pass route, which is handy for errands along the way.

Student Discounts (Tsugaku Teiki)

  • Cheaper than a regular commuter pass — Students get a steep discount on the route between home and school.
  • A student ID or certificate is required — Your school must issue a certificate proving your enrollment and route.
  • Must use the shortest reasonable route — Only the most direct path between home and school qualifies.
  • Ticket counters get busy each April — The start of the school year is the busiest time to apply — plan ahead.
Tip: International students can also use this discount with a certificate from their school — check with the school office.

Key Takeaways

  • Bus suits short local trips, subway suits fast city travel, and train suits long distances.
  • IC cards like PASMO and Suica work nationwide, so you can skip buying paper tickets.
  • The best IC card to get depends on the region you're in (ICOCA, Kitaca, etc.).
  • A commuter pass saves money if you regularly travel the same route.
  • Students can get an even cheaper pass with a certificate from their school.
