🚗 Converting a Foreign Driver's License to a Japanese License (Gaimen Kirikae) in 2026

Life in Japan Guide · Transportation

If you're planning to drive in Japan long-term, at some point you'll need to convert your foreign license into a Japanese one — a process known as gaimen kirikae (外免切替). Until recently this was fairly quick for people from many countries. That changed in October 2025, when Japan significantly tightened the rules. If you're going through this process in 2026, here's what's different and how to prepare.

Why the Rules Got Stricter

Gaimen kirikae applications had been rising fast — about 76,000 applications in 2024, 2.4 times what they were 10 years earlier. At the same time, safety concerns were growing: some license holders were converting to a Japanese license with little or no real skills testing. A serious hit-and-run case in Misato City, Saitama in May 2025, involving a foreign driver, became a turning point and pushed regulators to close the gap.

What Changed on October 1, 2025

  1. Proof of residency is now mandatory. You must submit a copy of your resident registration (juminhyo) to prove you're actually registered as living in Japan.
  2. Short-term visitors can no longer apply. Tourists and other short-term stayers are no longer eligible for gaimen kirikae (a small number of exceptions exist, such as diplomats and US military personnel stationed in Japan).
  3. The knowledge test got much harder. It used to be 10 true/false questions. It's now 50 questions, and you need at least 90% (45 correct) to pass. Topics include drunk driving and how to respond after an accident.
  4. The practical skills test got stricter. Examiners now specifically check things like how you handle crosswalks and railway crossings, and how you signal and execute turns — testing for realistic road scenarios rather than a simple loop.
  5. Fewer countries get exemptions. Only license holders from 29 countries/regions are still exempt from the full knowledge and skills confirmation (Indiana, USA is exempt from the skills test only). If your country isn't on that list, expect to sit both tests.

Documents You'll Typically Need

  • Your foreign driver's license (the physical card)
  • Passport (including old passports, if needed to show your entry/residency history)
  • Residence card
  • Copy of your resident registration (juminhyo) — new requirement
  • An official Japanese translation of your foreign license (available through JAF or your embassy/consulate, depending on the issuing country)
  • A medical certificate, in some cases
  • Additional documents may be requested depending on your local driver's license center — check before you go.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Confirm your eligibility — you generally need to have held and used your foreign license for at least 90 days in the country that issued it, after obtaining it.
  2. Gather your documents, including the official translation of your license.
  3. Apply at your prefectural driver's license center (not a driving school) for document screening.
  4. Take the knowledge confirmation test — 50 questions, 90% needed to pass.
  5. Take the practical skills test, unless your country is on the exemption list.
  6. Receive your Japanese license once you've passed all required stages.

Tips

  • Book early. Popular test centers are booking out months in advance — for example, Tokyo's Samezu license center had a roughly three-month wait in 2025. Don't leave this until you urgently need to drive.
  • Practice with real Japanese road rules, not just your home country's habits — things like stopping fully at railway crossings and yielding rules are common places people lose points.
  • Get your translation sorted early. JAF (Japan Automobile Federation) translations are widely accepted, but processing takes time.
  • Double-check the exempt-country list before you go — it can change, and it determines whether you'll need to sit the skills test at all.
